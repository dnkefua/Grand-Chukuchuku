# Grand-Chukuchuku — Chapter One Critic Standard

**Scope:** Episodes 1–12 of *Fiango*  
**Format:** 2 minutes 15 seconds to 2 minutes 45 seconds; vertical 9:16 animated hard-R drama  
**Purpose:** This is the pass/fail standard for the two writing drafts and all later revisions.

## The standard

The chapter must make an audience feel that Zino's adult choices come from a childhood shaped by intelligence, humor, money pressure, grief, friendship, education, witness to violence, and the possibility of escape. It cannot treat these as a list of unfortunate events. Each episode must change Zino's behavior, belief, relationship, or next choice.

The central dramatic engine is precise: Zino invents Grand-Chukuchuku, a man who can do anything, while real Zino repeatedly meets problems cleverness cannot solve. His recurring desire to build his mother a house gives the chapter a concrete emotional stake.

## Evidence and fact-tier rule

Every script page must identify one of these labels:

- **Confirmed recollection:** Zino directly supplied the fact or personal memory.
- **Family testimony:** Zino did not directly witness it; for example, his father's death when Zino was two.
- **Reported event:** Zino describes an event but dates, evidence, or other perspectives remain incomplete.
- **Proposed composite:** The scene arrangement, dialogue, timing, or merged event is a dramatic construction.

No proposed dialogue, invented motivation, or composite sequence may appear as confirmed autobiography. The accused in jungle-justice scenes remains accused, never silently established as guilty. Do not invent school names, grades, results, travel methods, characters' names, the mother's name, exact food, specific bullying acts, or the uncle's words.

## Vertical pacing rule

Every episode must earn 2:15–2:45 with one clean movement:

1. **0:00–0:15 — hook:** a striking image, argument, decision, sound, or pressure that can be understood without exposition.
2. **0:15–1:40 — escalation:** Zino wants something concrete; a person, rule, lack of money, or consequence blocks him; he chooses a response.
3. **1:40–2:45 — turn and hook:** that response produces an emotional or practical consequence, then opens the next episode.

Avoid speeches explaining Cameroon, poverty, faith, migration, or trauma. Use an object, rule, sound, bill, radio, school gate, exercise book, fence, or action instead. At least one shot per episode must work with sound off and subtitles on.

## Character tests

### Zino

He is witty, gifted, just, ambitious, hot-tempered, people-pleasing, and not fully self-aware. His wit must win something and cost something. His anger cannot be a decoration; it must force a choice. His justice instinct must become harder after jungle justice. His objective changes across the chapter: impress friends, protect his mother, stay in school, earn a promise, survive boarding school, and turn achievement into a future.

### Mother

She is a primary-school teacher, not only a symbol of sacrifice. Give her work, standards, humor, boundaries, professional competence, private fatigue, and at least one decision that materially changes Zino's path. Her belief in him must sometimes put pressure on him.

### Gideon

He is sharp, business-minded, quiet around girls, and buys his first camera in secondary school. His resources and hustle should complement Zino's social confidence, while exposing an unequal exchange in the friendship. Do not reduce him to comic relief or a future betrayer.

### Luc

He is God-fearing, carries a small Gideons Bible, preaches to students, and relies on Zino for maths and confidence around girls. His faith must produce comfort, embarrassment, courage, and eventually uncertainty when it cannot stop violence.

### Father as absence

He died when Zino was two. He can exist through family testimony, object, photograph, adult retelling, or contradiction between relatives. He cannot appear as Zino's direct memory.

## Grand-Chukuchuku device

Every fantasy sequence must reveal an unspoken need, lie, fear, or desire in Zino. It must be visually distinct from reality and brief enough to accelerate the episode. The device changes after “The Crowd”: Grand-Chukuchuku cannot solve public violence, and Zino's ability to tell the story should fracture or become quieter. Do not use the hero as a random joke or supernatural explanation.

## Fiango authenticity test

The compound should feel like a living system: two families, three single mothers, two bachelors, three student tenants, well, mango tree, rent, electricity, landlord, football, music, work, gossip, food, church, school routines, and neighborhood surveillance. The room should understand class through behavior and objects rather than speeches.

The first five school years are Fiango day school with Luc and Gideon. Professional School is a daytime vocational campus for building, construction, and electrical training; during June exam periods, classrooms open at night to day-school students. Some study; others socialize, smoke, drink, court girls, or escape home. Do not make every student delinquent or turn cultural specificity into shorthand.

The final two years are remote boarding high school, reached after a day of travel through mountains and valleys. The change in terrain, wealth, language, teachers, priests, discipline, bullying, food, and distance from home must be felt before it is explained.

## Hard-R jungle-justice test

Zino witnessed at least four jungle-justice incidents during day-school years. A hard-R composite can directly depict beating, four tires, fuel, fire, screaming, heat, and aftermath. The sequence must never become the show's coolest or most triumphant set piece. Its force comes from the crowd's certainty without proof, the boys' inability to act, and what Zino carries afterward: smoke, silence, anger, fear, or a damaged idea of justice. Violence requires a consequence in the next episode.

## Education and promise timeline

- Five Fiango day-school years end with Zino writing ten GCE Ordinary-Level subjects, from a maximum of eleven.
- Results are read on radio. The waiting and a name being called are communal events, not a private app notification.
- Proud of Zino's Ordinary-Level result, his uncle promises school choice and America if Zino succeeds in all five Advanced-Level subjects.
- Zino chooses the remote boarding school on that basis.
- Two boarding years end with Zino writing five GCE Advanced-Level subjects, the maximum and his university study area.
- The boarding school has a 100% Advanced-Level pass rate. The result scene's tension is public anticipation and the future that follows, not fear that Zino will fail.
- When Zino reminds his uncle about America, his uncle defers the promise until after Zino earns a first university degree.

The promise must move from pride, to motivation, to delay. It cannot be written as simple villainy or a casual plot device. The uncle's exact words and reasons remain unresolved.

## Ten-point fail/pass checklist

1. **Fact tier shown?** Every scene has a clear evidence label.
2. **Immediate want?** Zino wants something concrete within the first 15 seconds.
3. **Causal consequence?** His choice changes the end of the episode.
4. **Mother active?** She acts, decides, sets a boundary, or has a desire beyond supporting Zino.
5. **Friends distinct?** Gideon and Luc each pursue an independent want and never serve as interchangeable audience members.
6. **Grand-Chukuchuku earned?** The fantasy exposes Zino's reality rather than decorating it.
7. **Fiango or boarding specific?** At least two grounded cultural/environmental details affect action.
8. **Violence accountable?** Where hard-R violence appears, guilt remains unproven and aftermath affects the next beat.
9. **Timeline exact?** Day school, O Levels, boarding, A Levels, radio results, and uncle promise/deferment are in correct sequence.
10. **Hook earned?** The closing image or decision makes the next episode necessary, not merely mysterious.

An episode fails if it depends on exposition, invents a biographical fact, repeats a previous emotional beat, or turns Zino's suffering into a shortcut to importance. An episode passes only when its visual movement, dialogue, cultural detail, and emotional consequence all point in the same direction.
