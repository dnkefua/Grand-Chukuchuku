# Chapter 4: The Betrayal

**Period:** The venture dispute through deportation in 2019

**Focus:** A business dispute, the alleged report to immigration, deportation, and the struggle over what Zino helped build.

**Dramatic question:** When Zino can no longer remain in America, what can he preserve of his work, relationships, and sense of self?

**Beginning:** He has developed promising ventures but lacks appropriate immigration documents.

**Ending:** Deported in 2019, he returns to Cameroon with a sense of betrayal and the projects' actual fate still to establish.

## Act I - Setup

The ventures' possible future value changes the stakes of conversations among friends. Zino recalls discussing how much the work might one day be worth; he believes this encouraged an attempt to take it from him. Begin with what he knew at the time, allowing trust to become uncertain through the actual exchanges he remembers. Once the arrangements are clarified, scenes can explore disagreements over contribution, recognition, access, or ownership. His existing immigration vulnerability remains a separate pressure. Personal ambition and the friendship circle no longer feel safely aligned.

**Turning point:** People he trusted with his plans become people whose intentions he questions.

## Act II - Confrontation

Business uncertainty and immigration consequences converge in Zino's experience. He says friends reported him so that his absence would allow them to claim the venture. This is his account, with the source and evidence still to be established. Suspense comes from the limits of what he knows: whom to trust, what remains under his control, and what options are genuinely available. The sequence should follow his real immigration experience rather than supply an invented enforcement scene. Fear of losing his work becomes entangled with the prospect of leaving the country.

**Turning point:** The possible loss is no longer only financial: his place in America is ending.

## Act III - Consequence

Zino is deported in 2019 after remaining without appropriate documents. His departure does not by itself establish that Nkap or njangi.xyz was stolen; access, ownership, continuity, and losses need their own factual account. He returns carrying interrupted ambitions, the family's investment, and a sense of betrayal. A homecoming scene can make space for his mother's real response, rather than assume disappointment or blame. America has ended as a place he can remain, but the obligation he feels toward his future and his family has not disappeared.

**Turning point:** The next question is how to begin again without the life he expected to keep.

## Possible short episodes

1. **Who Owns What?:** A real disagreement exposes different understandings of participation and control, once those arrangements are known.
2. **Who Told You?:** Zino learns of the alleged report; the episode follows the actual source and limits of that information.
3. **Back Home:** A remembered return conversation tests what failure, success, and family loyalty mean after deportation.
