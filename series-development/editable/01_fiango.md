# Chapter 1: Fiango

**Period:** Around 2000-2007, with earlier memories clearly identified

**Focus:** Football, friendship, unpaid school fees, and a gifted boy's growing determination to give his mother a better life.

**Dramatic question:** What happens when a boy who can invent a solution to anything meets a problem he cannot talk his way out of?

**Beginning:** At ten, Zino has completed primary school and lives with his mother in Fiango.

**Ending:** Secondary school is complete in 2007; his ambition now depends on finding a way to continue.

## Act I - Setup

Street football introduces Zino's wit, competitiveness, and temper. Luc and Gideon hear his outrageous stories about Grand-Chukuchuku, the man who achieves what nobody else can. Brief fantasy sequences make those boasts visible before returning us to the real game. The compound is a small society: two families, three single mothers, two bachelors, and three student tenants share the well and mango tree. The landlord's monthly collection of rent and electricity money turns money into a recurring presence. Zino's mother, a primary-school teacher, believes in him. His father died after a snake bite while spraying cocoa when Zino was two, so the loss enters through family history rather than a memory he owns.

**Turning point:** His imagination can win an argument, but the household's limits cannot be wished away.

## Act II - Confrontation

Education reveals both Zino's ability and the limits of that advantage. His earlier primary-school fee exclusion can return as a clearly placed memory; the later high-school exclusion belongs at its actual date. His mother's primary-school teaching income and his uncle's intermittent help keep opportunity alive without removing uncertainty. Boarding school in Kumba supplies comic escapes over the fence to eat at home. Each June, the daytime vocational school called Professional School opens classrooms at night for secondary-school students to revise, study in groups, socialize, or escape home; show its temptations without claiming every student is distracted. Zino's four reported encounters with jungle justice occur in these years. Feature them through a composite episode about fear and aftermath, not graphic spectacle.

**Turning point:** Being the cleverest boy in the room does not guarantee that he can remain in it.

## Act III - Consequence

Zino completes secondary school in 2007. Academic progress creates a new threshold rather than financial security. The closing movement should grow from a remembered examination, result, or family conversation, making clear what this achievement means to him and his mother. Grand-Chukuchuku can still supply effortless victories; Zino's real future requires help, patience, and choices. The child who dreams of lifting his mother out of hardship now faces the practical requirements of further education. The chapter ends with a milestone achieved and the next opportunity still uncertain.

**Turning point:** He has the ability to continue, but the household still needs a way to pay.

## Possible short episodes

1. **Grand-Chukuchuku Never Loses:** A football dispute introduces the imaginary hero and the real boy's difficulty accepting an unfavorable decision.
2. **School Fees:** At the remembered age, a fee exclusion interrupts academic confidence and changes the rhythm of an ordinary school day.
3. **Professional School:** June evening revision creates a divided space: some students study; some pursue distraction; Zino must decide what kind of future he is preparing for.
4. **The Crowd:** After Zino witnesses the aftermath of an alleged thief being attacked, the episode stays with his fear, the adults' silence, and the question of justice rather than depicting the killing.
