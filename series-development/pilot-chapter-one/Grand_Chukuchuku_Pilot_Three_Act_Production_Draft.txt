GRAND CHUKUCHUKU
Two-minute pilot - three-act production draft

The boys have completed primary school and are on holiday before starting secondary school. All three are going to GBHS Kumba. Luc would have preferred SASSE, but his parents cannot afford to sponsor him there. The author's dialogue below is retained exactly; the tenant lines are proposed for his review. The times are targets and must be checked against a natural table read.

ACT ONE: THE ROAD AND THE FRIENDS (0:00-0:43)

On the road between the established compound and Professional School, Zino taps a football as he meets Gideon and Luc. At least three other children are actively playing; Gideon and Luc watch. Tenants are visible around the compound. Pedestrians, bicycles and motorbikes pass on the road.

ZINO: Giddy! Giddy. Wah Pastor Lucky Luc! Blood deh my eye. Which school wuna d go? Me I do GBHS up station.

GIDEON: Zino, you and this ball! Why you nor go Kadji Sport for play football? Me to na GB I d go. How for you Luc?

LUC: Same. Doe nor deh like I for like for go SASSE.

ZINO: SASSE weh ngah them nor deh? Never! Even if I want go bording school I must go na mix. Massa ma nack small ndamba.

PLAYER: Bike! Make we wait!

The players stop and wait for the bike and pedestrians to pass, then resume. Keep the road lively and the compound geography consistent.

ACT TWO: THE FUTURE THEY IMAGINE (0:43-1:23)

The conversation continues as neighbourhood life carries on around them. Luc has a brief disappointed beat when he mentions SASSE; the financial reason is part of the story background and is not invented as new dialogue.

ZINO: Man go start sap like professional. Jacket and long trousers. Norbi that short nicka for primary school.

LUC: Population and distance for waka. Chai under hot sun.

GIDEON: Zino I hear say fine fine French girls them deh that school. Just make sure say ya man get one I beg.

ZINO: Nor worry Giddi make we first reach Ju Je the atmosphere.

GIDEON: Secondary school boys now!

LUC: New school, new teachers... everything new.

ZINO: Wuna think say secondary school go hard?

GIDEON: If e hard, you go explain am for we!

ZINO: For free?

They laugh. Across the road a tenant notices someone approaching and quietly starts closing a window. The boys have not yet noticed. The audience senses a change before the reveal.

ACT THREE: THE LANDLORD ARRIVES (1:23-2:00)

Zino spots the landlord approaching the compound. His expression changes. The reaction spreads across multiple households: people hurry into rooms, close doors and windows, and some try to leave. Other tenants remain visible watching from doorways and windows. Street sounds continue underneath the sudden tension.

ZINO: Wait... na landlord.

GIDEON: E dey come for compound?

Two tenants quietly coach their children. These additional lines are proposed, not author-approved dialogue:

TENANT ONE: If landlord ask, tell am say I no dey.

CHILD: But Ma...

TENANT ONE: Shhh! Just tell am.

TENANT TWO: Tell landlord say I don go.

ZINO: Give me one minute I d cam.

Zino runs toward his mother's room. His friends watch from the road. The landlord reaches the compound entrance, ledger and keys in hand.

ZINO: Ma! Landlord dee come!

Cut to the landlord at the gate as Zino's warning finishes. Cut to black before Mother answers, a knock is answered, or anyone confronts the landlord. The next episode opens on the first day at school; it does not begin by replaying the landlord encounter.

PRODUCTION CONTINUITY AND SOUND

- Match the accepted compound layout opposite Professional School, including the road, the arrangement of rooms, the mango tree and the well. Use the established setting reference consistently across shots.
- Keep tenants visible in compound shots, including during the landlord sequence. Their evasive behaviour creates the tension; do not invent violence or a confrontation.
- Whenever Zino has the ball on the street, show at least three other active players. Gideon and Luc watch, rather than play. Play pauses for passing people and bikes.
- Do not show Grand Chukuchuku as an image, portrait, silhouette or fantasy figure anywhere in this pilot.
- Carry ball, voices, footsteps and traffic naturally between shots. Let closing doors, whispered instructions, keys and the well rope raise tension without cutting all neighbourhood ambience.
- Use approved performance tracks for the dialogue and align visible mouth movements to those tracks. Use reaction shots and cutaways to cover difficult transitions. Do not alter the author's Pidgin spelling without his approval.
