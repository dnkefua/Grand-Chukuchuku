## 1. A weekday has its own ticking clock

### 5:30 a.m. — the cocks behind his room

**C:** Zino wakes to cock-crowing behind his room around 5:30. Four buckets come before school: two rounds to the well, one full bucket in each hand on each return.

**P — screen treatment:** Show only closed doors and feet from the ordinary queue's viewpoint. An impatient toe tapping outside answers still feet inside. No camera enters an occupied cubicle; there is no nudity. Whether this exact door detail applies to bathrooms, toilets or both remains open.

### Two rooms, not one

**C:** Zino has his own room, two apartments detached from his mother's room toward the front. Her room contains a queen-size bed and a drawer beside it. After getting ready for school, he enters her room to check the money left in that drawer.

**C — later addition:** Mother trusts him. The separation between their rooms means he can come and go or sleep late without her always knowing. This is freedom within their particular living arrangement; it does not establish neglect, permission for every activity, or her constant absence.

**P — screen treatment:** The route between rooms creates private and public space: he must pass other tenants before reaching Mother. ### The 200-CFA decision

**C:** Usually two coins totalling 200 CFA are left for school. He can use 100 for transport and spend 100 at school. If he gets ready early enough to walk, he can keep the 200 for school. Earlier recollections establish that he sometimes saves lunch money for Nacking Bottle.

**Clarified:** a single taxi ride costs 100 CFA from the pickup to school, on three mornings per week. It is not three connecting rides. Two coins totalling 200 are confirmed; exact denominations need not be drawn. Exact weekdays and travel time remain open.

**P — dramatic engine:** Time, effort, hunger and football all draw on the same small budget. A late bathroom queue can change his transport decision. An early start can buy a better school day. That is a real choice; it does not need an invented criminal threat.

## 6. How to use this in a thriller series
