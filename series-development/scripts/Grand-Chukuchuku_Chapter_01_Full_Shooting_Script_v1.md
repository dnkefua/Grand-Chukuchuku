# GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU
## Chapter One: **Fiango** — Full Shooting Script v1

**Format:** 12 x 2:15–2:45 episodes; original adult 2D animation; vertical 9:16; English and Cameroon Pidgin with English subtitles.  
**Chapter span:** Primary-school memory (1999), Fiango day-school years (2000–2005), and boarding-school years (2005–2007). The approximate years are derived from Zino's supplied 1999 primary completion, five day-school years, two boarding years, and 2007 secondary completion.  
**Dialogue rule:** Every line of dialogue below is **P — Proposed composite dialogue** unless quoted by Zino in a later revision. It is written for performance, not as a claimed transcript.

### Provenance key

- **C — Confirmed recollection:** fact supplied directly by Zino.
- **FT — Family testimony:** a fact Zino did not personally witness, including his father's death when Zino was two.
- **R — Reported / incomplete:** a supplied event whose particular date, people, order, or method is still incomplete.
- **P — Proposed composite:** scene construction, image, chronology within a period, action, or dialogue created for drama. It must not be presented in marketing as literal autobiography.

## One-page chapter arc

At ten, **Nkeh “Zino” Nkengzincho** survives Fiango by being the clever boy with the fastest mouth. Football, the well, mango tree and two close friends make him feel protected. When rent and light pressure prove too adult for a joke, he makes up **Grand-Chukuchuku**: an impossible version of himself who can win any argument, goal or battle. The invention hides one plain desire—to build his mother a house and keep money from cornering her again.

Across five Fiango day-school years, Gideon's camera asks whether Zino helps a friend or takes his spotlight; Luc's faith asks whether truth is useful when it costs social comfort; and Zino's fee exclusion proves ability cannot always buy a place in class. His mother teaches at school and at home, gives him standards, and makes difficult choices without promising miracles. His paternal uncle's occasional fee support is welcome but irregular.

Night revision at Professional School holds work, escape and danger in the same corridors. One jungle-justice incident Zino recalls breaks Grand-Chukuchuku's certainty: the accused man's guilt is unknown, yet public violence is real. Zino turns back to study, writes ten GCE Ordinary-Level subjects, and hears his result on the radio. His proud uncle promises school choice and America if Zino makes all five A-Level papers.

Zino chooses a remote boarding school. Mountains, distance, wealth, white teachers and priests, strict routine, hunger, bullying and the fence replace Fiango's open compound. After the maximum five A-Level subjects and a radio result at a school with a 100% pass rate, Zino has met the condition. His uncle then moves America to after a first university degree. Achievement has opened the next gate, not removed it.

**Grand-Chukuchuku rule:** Zino tells a story before he sees the hero. The hero appears only when the story protects a desire or a fear. He is exuberant in Episode 1, absent when real work is enough, silent before the crowd in Episode 8, and reduced to a shadow at the promise's deferment in Episode 12.
