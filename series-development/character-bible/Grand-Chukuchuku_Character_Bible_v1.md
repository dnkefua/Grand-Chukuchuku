# 01 — Zino and Grand-Chukuchuku

**Visual sheet:** `images/01_Zino_and_Grand.png`

### NKEH “ZINO” NKENGZINCHO

**Story role:** The gifted boy who can turn defeat into a story, then must learn that wit cannot solve every material problem.

- **C — Foundation:** Born 1990; in Chapter One’s opening he is ten in Fiango, Kumba. He is intelligent, witty, kind and strongly drawn to justice. He speaks Pidgin with friends, English at home, and French and English at school. He wants to build his mother a house. His weaknesses include temper, people-pleasing and limited self-awareness.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Slim, quick-footed silhouette; short tapered curls; bright, watchful eyes; expressive brow; faded white football vest, worn blue shorts and bare feet for street play. His face must carry the same brow, hairline and quick smile as Grand’s.
- **Performance:** Words arrive before caution. When Zino is frightened, reduce the speed of his speech rather than remove his humour. His anger is hot and immediate, but never his entire personality.
- **Props:** Scuffed ball, school bag, folded exercise-book page, small coins, well bucket.
- **Do not do:** Do not turn cleverness into invulnerability, poverty into costume, or childhood into an adult body language.

### GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU

**Story role:** Zino’s fictional ideal man—the person in his impossible stories who can do what nobody else can.

- **C — Foundation:** Grand-Chukuchuku is Zino’s invented ideal person. He is not a literal future Zino, a ghost or a superhero. He appears when Zino actively makes a story border reality and fiction.
- **C — Locked baseline:** Short tapered curls, close beard, warm brown complexion, navy suit, open white shirt, brown shoes and a composed, expressive bearing come from the author-supplied model sheet.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Navy, cream and precise gold reflections; stillness before action; controlled eye line; one hand in a pocket or relaxed at his side. His fantasy entrance alters light and motion, not physical reality.
- **Performance:** He does not chatter. He is confident because Zino needs him to be. As the story darkens, he appears less often and can fail to arrive.
- **Do not do:** No flying, capes, superpowers, combat poses or literal rescue of real-world consequences.

# 02 — Gideon, Luc and Elianne

**Visual sheet:** `images/02_Friends_and_Elianne.png`

### GIDEON

**Story role:** The business-minded friend who sees the price, the opportunity and the camera angle before others do.

- **C — Foundation:** Gideon is sharp, focused and always looking for ways to make money. He bought his first camera when he went to secondary school, booked birthday-party camera gigs, often paid for small outings with Zino, and liked Zino’s help approaching girls because Gideon was quiet and found it hard to keep a conversation going.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Compact, guarded posture; olive-green striped polo over school trousers or practical shorts; cross-body camera bag; a small period-appropriate camera becomes his identifying prop. His gaze should measure a room before he enters it.
- **Performance:** His jokes land dry. He speaks only after he has calculated the situation. When he laughs freely, the audience should feel that trust has been earned.
- **Do not do:** Do not make him a comic coward or a sidekick whose only purpose is to pay bills.

### LUC

**Story role:** The moral pressure inside the friendship group: faith, fairness and honesty without self-righteousness.

- **C — Foundation:** Luc is God-fearing, carries a small Bible, preaches to fellow students, and values Zino’s honesty, humour, help with mathematics and conversations about girls.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Lean upright silhouette; neat white school shirt, brown shorts or trousers; compact worn Bible close to his body. His stillness should contrast with Zino’s restless movement.
- **Performance:** Clear and direct. He has courage to speak in public even when nobody listens. His faith should guide action, not produce speeches in every scene.
- **Do not do:** Never reduce Luc to a punchline, a hypocrite or an automatic judge of his friends.

### ELIANNE

**Story role:** Zino’s first love and a person whose life must not be reduced to being desired by the lead.

- **C — Foundation:** Elianne is identified as Zino’s first love. Her exact age, years, school, relationship arc and voice have not yet been supplied.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Thoughtful school-era peer with neat braids, a ribbon, books and a deliberate way of holding a conversation. Blue is her recurring colour, distinct from Zino’s earth tones, Gideon’s olive, and Luc’s white/brown.
- **Performance:** Give her private wants, friendships, competence and boundaries before any romantic scene. Her attention cannot be a prize for Zino’s charm.
- **Continuity hold:** Keep this design provisional until Zino records when and how they met, her age then, what she wanted, and what the relationship changed.

# 03 — Zino’s family

**Visual sheet:** `images/03_Family.png`

### ZINO’S MOTHER

**Story role:** The person Zino most wants to provide for; a teacher whose work and faith in him make the dream concrete.

- **C — Foundation:** She raised Zino as a single mother after his father’s death, taught in primary school from a young age and throughout his childhood, believed in Zino, and carried household pressures alongside teaching.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Upright posture, patterned teacher’s dresses, practical shoes, shoulder bag and a stack of marked exercise books. Warmth belongs in a private smile or a quiet touch, not in a softened version of her workload.
- **Performance:** She does not need raised volume to hold a scene. Let pauses, a corrected exercise book, a bucket set down carefully, and the decision to keep going carry her strength.
- **Do not do:** Do not turn her into suffering alone. She needs humour, professional skill, friends, limits and a point of view that is not only about Zino.

### ZINO’S PATERNAL UNCLE

**Story role:** An intermittent financial support, a proud witness to Zino’s academic achievement, and the source of a promise that changes shape.

- **C — Foundation:** He helped with school fees from time to time. Proud of Zino’s Ordinary-Level results, he promised Zino could choose any school and go to America if he passed all five Advanced Levels. After Zino completed them, the uncle deferred America until after Zino earned a first university degree.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Older than Zino’s mother, understated collared shirt, folded newspaper, plain envelope, careful hands. His visual language is modest responsibility, never automatic wealth.
- **Performance:** Pride can coexist with constraint. Do not play the promise’s deferral as cartoon betrayal until Zino provides the uncle’s reasons and the actual conversation.
- **Continuity hold:** Name, profession, family circumstances, exact contribution, and motive for the deferral remain open.

### ZINO’S FATHER

**Story role:** A loss Zino inherited rather than remembered.

- **FT — Foundation:** Zino’s father died from a snake bite while spraying cocoa when Zino was two. Family testimony says he cut the area and tried to suck the venom before passing out and dying before reaching home. Zino has no direct recollection.
- **P — Visual treatment:** Use only a respectful framed photograph, family-told memory or restrained symbolic image until a relative’s account and any real visual reference are supplied. The current visual sheet’s cocoa-farmer portrait is nonliteral.
- **Performance / writing rule:** Never give him remembered dialogue or a first-person flashback from Zino’s point of view. His absence should be felt in family stories and in the life it left behind.
- **Do not do:** Do not show the injury for spectacle or invent his personality, voice, fault or final moment.

# 04 — School and compound adults

**Visual sheet:** `images/04_School_and_Compound_Adults.png`

### MISS MUKETE

**Story role:** Zino’s favourite chemistry teacher and a potential adult who sees his academic strength clearly.

- **C — Foundation:** Miss Mukete is Zino’s favourite chemistry teacher. No additional biography, speech patterns, physical description or classroom memory has been confirmed yet.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Gold-rimmed reading glasses, neat low natural hairstyle, blue patterned blouse under a white lab coat, a chemistry notebook and a small beaker. Blue, chalk white and glass reflections separate her from the more earth-toned Fiango palette.
- **Performance:** Precise, observant and encouraging. She should demand work without becoming a generic inspirational teacher.
- **Continuity hold:** Her likeness, age, exact school, chemistry routines and relationship with Zino need his memories before an episode gives her a personal storyline.

### THE LANDLORD

**Story role:** The recurring face of monthly rent and electricity pressure in the compound.

- **C — Foundation:** The Fiango compound contains two families, three single mothers, two bachelors and three student tenants. The landlord comes each month to collect house rent and electricity money.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Lean older man, silver at the temples, pale short-sleeve shirt, dark trousers, leather sandals, keys and a well-used ledger. His measured face lets the audience read the money pressure without claiming he is cruel.
- **Performance:** Brief, factual, unshowy. He belongs to the compound’s economy, but his own circumstances have not been supplied.
- **Do not do:** Do not make him a villain, establish his motives, invent an amount owed or present a specific rent confrontation as fact.

## Cast-wide production rules

1. Keep every visual difference tied to character action: Zino moves first, Gideon scans, Luc squares himself, Mother holds the room, Uncle weighs, Miss Mukete observes, and the Landlord records.
2. Preserve the supplied compound and Grand-Chukuchuku references as visual authorities. New clothes, age variants and locations may evolve, but Zino and Grand retain their family resemblance.
3. A proposed physical feature is a production choice, not a claim about a real person. Replace it when Zino supplies a real reference, correction or fictionalization direction.
4. Do not stage the father’s death, the aunt’s killing, or the alleged immigration report as fact until source details and the appropriate point of view are established.
