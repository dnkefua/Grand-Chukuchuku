# Chapter 2: The Price of Leaving

**Period:** 2007-2012

**Focus:** Education, first love, family tensions, and the sale of land that makes America possible.

**Dramatic question:** How does Zino pursue his future when the opportunity depends on sacrifices made by people he loves?

**Beginning:** Secondary school is complete; further education offers a route toward his ambitions.

**Ending:** After his first degree in 2011, support and a land sale make departure for America possible in 2012.

## Act I - Setup

Zino enters the next stage of his education with ability, ambition, and financial uncertainty. The dream of America begins to require concrete qualifications and preparations. His mother believes in him; his paternal uncle remains an important source of help. Elianne's place in this period should follow the actual relationship timeline, bringing intimacy and a competing sense of what a future might look like. Study, romance, family expectations, and the dream of nursing belong together only where his memories connect them. Childhood confidence must now become a workable plan.

**Turning point:** America becomes a practical possibility rather than a story about someday.

## Act II - Confrontation

Completing his first degree in 2011 brings Zino closer to departure, but does not settle its cost. His uncle, friends, and family contribute, and a large piece of family land is sold. This is the chapter's central material sacrifice: an existing asset is exchanged for an uncertain opportunity abroad. Land and money are already sources of family tension in his account; the specific participants and disagreements still need his memories. Reconstructed conversations should reveal what each person hoped the contribution would accomplish, without assuming conditions or promises nobody made.

**Turning point:** The journey now carries other people's resources and expectations as well as Zino's ambition.

## Act III - Consequence

In 2012, preparation becomes departure. Packing, farewell, and travel should draw their emotional detail from what Zino actually remembers. He expects American study to be manageable and looks toward the opportunities ahead; he does not yet know how familiar the financial pressure will feel there. The chapter lets excitement coexist with the cost of leaving people behind. A remembered exchange with his mother can connect his immediate destination to the larger wish to provide for her. End as Cameroon becomes the home he must learn to carry at a distance.

**Turning point:** Reaching America will fulfill the travel dream, but it will not settle how to build a secure life.
