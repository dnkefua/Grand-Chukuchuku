**Date:** 30 October 2026.  
**Scope:** The author's latest Luc, Gideon, Zino, Tracey, examination and home-life additions, checked against active Chapter One and pilot sources, character-bible generation and the series outline. This is editorial review of author-supplied recollections and proposed dramatization, not independent biographical or examination-board verification.

**Verdict:** Revised written sources, updated friend portrait and all three revised pilot sheets pass for author review. This approves the present development revision, not a finished animation or a production lock. Earlier critic approvals were not substituted for inspection of the new images.

## The changes that matter dramatically

Luc's strength and Gideon's enterprise give the friendship an immediate, readable contrast. Luc trains in the evening using mud-formed dumbbells and a sand-filled punching bag; his high flat-top and muscular older-secondary silhouette supersede the earlier lean character concept. Gideon's W-shaped hairline, almost-afro hair and relentless gigs distinguish him before a camera bag is visible. His Form Five bread-selling place is a specific development in his enterprise, not permission to place that stall in every earlier year.

Zino's attraction to maths is pleasure and confidence: he experiences it as a puzzle, unlike subjects requiring more reading. This must not become a claim that he cannot read or never studies. Singing, songwriting from Form Five and a first demo after boarding-school homecoming establish a musical life before America. His Catholic faith survives the secondary-school workload that ends his primary-school altar service.

Mother's trust is the emotional counterweight to the separate room. Greater freedom of movement creates choices and consequences; it does not establish neglect, approval of every outing or permanent absence of supervision. Tracey's two unsuccessful GCE sittings and her observation of Zino helping pupils who later passed establish school context. They do not prove her exact age, private motives, a transaction, or what caused every pupil's success. The school-age account remains neutral and non-graphic.

## Initial audit findings

1. The active character-bible builder and its Markdown replacement map still reproduced a lean Luc. Repairing only the appendix would leave the PDF's core character card and generated Markdown in conflict.
2. The earlier active portrait and pilot sheets predate the muscular/high-flat-top and W-hairline corrections. Source text alone cannot make those drawings current. Revised sheets require actual visual inspection, including childhood proportions and the existing sky-blue/navy uniform.
3. The series outline's “Dates to place” row combined later family-crisis losses with school-era witness incidents. It should split the periods explicitly rather than leave a modifier that can attach to the whole list.
4. Added character facts should earn their place through ordinary actions and choices. A two-minute episode loses force if the new context becomes a catalogue recited by the cast.
5. A wider script sweep found Grand appearing without Zino actively telling a story in Episodes 15, 34, 43, 48, 50, 56, 59 and 60. This broke the established device and, at the crowd and results, displaced the real boy's experience. Those beats were rejected and revised. The final uncle-conversation montage also needed to stop implying a continuous, unconfirmed return journey through the mountain school.
