# 01 — Zino and Grand-Chukuchuku

**Visual sheet:** `images/01_Zino_and_Grand.png`

### NKEH “ZINO” NKENGZINCHO

**Story role:** The gifted boy who can turn defeat into a story, then must learn that wit cannot solve every material problem.

- **C — Foundation:** Born 1990; in Chapter One’s opening he is ten in Fiango, Kumba. He is intelligent, witty, kind and strongly drawn to justice. He speaks Pidgin with friends, English at home, and French and English at school. He wants to build his mother a house. His weaknesses include temper, people-pleasing and limited self-awareness.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Slim, quick-footed silhouette; short tapered curls; bright, watchful eyes; expressive brow; faded white football vest, worn blue shorts and bare feet for street play. His face must carry the same brow, hairline and quick smile as Grand’s.
- **Performance:** Words arrive before caution. When Zino is frightened, reduce the speed of his speech rather than remove his humour. His anger is hot and immediate, but never his entire personality.
- **Props:** Scuffed ball, school bag, folded exercise-book page, small coins, well bucket.
- **Do not do:** Do not turn cleverness into invulnerability, poverty into costume, or childhood into an adult body language.

### GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU

**Story role:** Zino’s fictional ideal man—the person in his impossible stories who can do what nobody else can.

- **C — Foundation:** Grand-Chukuchuku is Zino’s invented ideal person. He is not a literal future Zino, a ghost or a superhero. He appears when Zino actively makes a story border reality and fiction.
- **C — Locked baseline:** Short tapered curls, close beard, warm brown complexion, navy suit, open white shirt, brown shoes and a composed, expressive bearing come from the author-supplied model sheet.
- **P — Distinct visual code:** Navy, cream and precise gold reflections; stillness before action; controlled eye line; one hand in a pocket or relaxed at his side. His fantasy entrance alters light and motion, not physical reality.
- **Performance:** He does not chatter. He is confident because Zino needs him to be. As the story darkens, he appears less often and can fail to arrive.
- **Do not do:** No flying, capes, superpowers, combat poses or literal rescue of real-world consequences.

## 1. A weekday has its own ticking clock

### 5:30 a.m. — the cocks behind his room

**C:** Zino wakes to cock-crowing behind his room around 5:30. Four buckets come before school: two rounds to the well, one full bucket in each hand on each return.

**P — screen treatment:** Begin with sound in darkness, then a low shot of two bucket handles pulling down on his hands. The first round is not the last. A wide shot establishes the actual route from his detached room to the shared well. This can show fatigue, humour and household duty before anyone explains poverty.

### The bathroom queue

**C:** Tenants share two bathrooms. Zino waits his turn. Feet and lower shins are visible under the short door, telling someone outside that it is occupied.

**P — screen treatment:** Show only closed doors and feet from the ordinary queue's viewpoint. An impatient toe tapping outside answers still feet inside. No camera enters an occupied cubicle; there is no nudity. Whether this exact door detail applies to bathrooms, toilets or both remains open.

### Two rooms, not one

**C:** Zino has his own room, two apartments detached from his mother's room toward the front. Her room contains a queen-size bed and a drawer beside it. After getting ready for school, he enters her room to check the money left in that drawer.

**P — screen treatment:** The route between rooms creates private and public space: he must pass other tenants before reaching Mother. Do not invent a locked door, secret drawer, missing cash or theft. Checking the money is his routine, not automatically a suspicious act. Exact room dimensions and the age at which this arrangement began are open.

### The 200-CFA decision

**C:** Usually two coins totalling 200 CFA are left for school. He can use 100 for transport and spend 100 at school. If he gets ready early enough to walk, he can keep the 200 for school. Earlier recollections establish that he sometimes saves lunch money for Nacking Bottle.

**Clarified:** a single taxi ride costs 100 CFA from the pickup to school, on three mornings per week. It is not three connecting rides. Two coins totalling 200 are confirmed; exact denominations need not be drawn. Exact weekdays and travel time remain open.

**P — dramatic engine:** Time, effort, hunger and football all draw on the same small budget. A late bathroom queue can change his transport decision. An early start can buy a better school day. That is a real choice; it does not need an invented criminal threat.

## 6. How to use this in a thriller series

The extraordinary events need an ordinary world worth losing. Suspense can grow from a specific choice and a visible consequence; every bucket, TV programme and friendship does not need a sinister score.

### Proposed three-act short seeds

1. **Four Buckets:** I — the cocks wake him and he wants an early start. II — two full rounds take work. III — the bathroom queue makes him calculate whether walking is still possible. Do not add a real accident without a memory.
2. **The Feet Under the Door:** I — Zino joins the queue. II — neighbours arrive with different schedules. III — his turn changes the morning's time budget. Keep framing outside occupied bathrooms.
3. **Two Coins:** I — the drawer contains 200 CFA. II — transport means spending half; walking costs time. III — choose one and carry that consequence into school.
4. **The Long Walk:** I — he leaves early enough. II — he passes ordinary neighbourhood activity. III — he reaches school with the money intact. Walking is a practical trade, not a magical cure for school fees.
5. **Gideon's Seat:** I — Zino needs a good game. II — Gideon narrates his mistakes from the sideline. III — Zino loses and must decide how to take a joke without turning on a friend.
6. **Two Evenings:** I — football attracts Zino. II — Luc finds someone willing to talk. III — their routes reconnect without either friend needing to imitate the other.
7. **Through the Bars:** I — children gather at the TV window. II — the view is narrow and a match or film matters. III — access becomes part of the pleasure and frustration. No invented ban or malicious owner is required.
8. **Ayuk's TV:** I — during secondary school, the group has a new place to watch. II — the evening grows late. III — 9 p.m. means home and tomorrow's 5:30 start. Exact form/year of purchase remains open.
9. **Mirabel's Sum:** I — a maths question starts a friendship in Form Three. II — mutual respect grows through repeated work. III — give her a response and an independent want; friendship need not become romance.
10. **Bro Code:** I — Gideon hopes Sophy will choose him. II — she approaches Zino instead. III — he refuses without publicly humiliating her or congratulating himself.
11. **The Store:** I — Zino sees his uncle's cocoa operation. II — the uncle urges education while work continues. III — Zino hears the reference to his father and carries a difficult question home. This is proposed staging; no warehouse visit is confirmed.
12. **Form Three:** I — Lizzy's connection with Zino matters. II — attention and money complicate it. III — the breakup happens in Form Three; record the exact sequence before adding Tracey's experience as an aftermath.
