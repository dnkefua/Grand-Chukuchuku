# GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU — Pilot rebuild: director and camera brief

**Status:** Proposed direction for the writer/critic team, not a verified account of one particular evening.  
**Purpose:** Replace the old reference-art layout with a storyboard in which every depicted camera position, action and reaction earns its place.  
**Working sequence:** A secondary-school-age Fiango evening moving from football and home to Professional School night revision, a crowd event and its emotional aftermath. Exact staging and words are proposed composites.

## What the original board failed to deliver

The previous document names seventeen boards but repeatedly displays the same handful of reference pictures. A photograph-like character anchor cannot illustrate a ball grazing a slipper, a rushed bucket pull, the landlord passing, the school lunch bell and a coin being hidden. Those are separate events with separate compositions. A caption describing a rack focus does not make a still picture demonstrate its initial and final focal planes.

The result is illustrated prose, not a sequence from which an animation team can understand screen direction, eyeline, action, reaction, cause and consequence. The replacement must show the intended shot itself. Beautiful key art can remain on a cover; it cannot substitute for the board sequence.

The dramatic camera must also have an engine. Before each cut ask: **what new thing can the audience know, fear or misunderstand after this shot?** A shot that adds nothing beyond a colour or pose should be removed.

## Locked story and chronology after critic review

The revised pilot is a **frame narrative**, not a single-period age-ten story. It opens for twelve seconds at the well with fourteen-year-old Zino unable to answer Mother's “What happened?” It then cuts on **FOUR YEARS EARLIER** to ten-year-old Zino playing and inventing Grand. At 0:50, **FOUR YEARS LATER — EARLIER THAT EVENING** takes us to the secondary-school night-study sequence that will answer the opening. The ending returns to the actual cold-open moment, not a second unrelated threat.

The age-ten passage establishes the essential claim: **“Grand no ever run.”** The older Zino must choose to take both friends out of the crowd. At home, he starts “Grand…” but cannot finish the fictional rescue; he says **“There was a man.”** The character has changed the way he tells the truth. Mother speaks English at home.

Use “approximately 2004” only in production metadata until Zino confirms the event year. Born 1990 gives age ten approximately 2000 and age fourteen approximately 2004. This remains inside the five day-school years before boarding. Gideon has **no camera in the age-ten passage**; the older Gideon may carry the camera he acquired in secondary school.

Do not carry over the previous primary-school fee warning: birth in 1990 and primary completion in 1999 make a present-tense age-ten primary exclusion contradictory. The confirmed primary-fee incident can enter through a labelled memory once its timing is supplied.

The threat comes from the already reported witness experience. No kidnapping, secret killer, stolen evidence, police chase, manufactured outside knock, or imaginary rescue is added.

## Spatial contract

Build a simple top-down layout before the first panel. The author’s compound image remains the material and geography baseline.

- **Home court:** Mango tree centrally behind the well. Mother’s doorway is one stable screen landmark. Establish the compound entrance before anyone needs to get out of it.
- **Road:** Football pitch and the route to Professional School share a directional spine. Departures from home move screen-left to screen-right; returns move right to left. This is a production choice, not a claim about the actual compass direction.
- **Professional School:** A practical building/electrical/construction campus. Show a real workshop or board with a wiring/building diagram before night study begins. The gate is a known landmark before the crowd blocks it.
- **Crowd sequence:** The camera stays on the boys’ side of the street. It never jumps to an omniscient attacker’s view. The audience knows only what they can see and hear. Establish a possible exit before bodies close the foreground gap.
- **Home return:** Reuse the same doorway composition, with different posture and light. Familiar geography becomes emotionally different without a new location.

## Camera grammar

### Warmth and charisma

Use a low 28–35 mm-equivalent viewpoint for Zino in football play, close enough to exaggerate feet and the ball. Keep horizon level. Bodies can break the frame because the world initially feels open. Reserve a genuine high overview to show the boys’ small size against the court. Do not let every scene become a smiling portrait at waist level.

### Imagination

Enter Grand through a motivated visual match: Zino’s moving hand becomes Grand’s composed hand; a scuffed ball’s arc becomes a gold trail; a roof edge becomes a poised stage. Grand is adult, navy-suited, expressive and human in silhouette. The fantasy can describe an impossible feat, but the board must make it clear that it is Zino’s active invention. Return to the real world through the exact object that began the story.

Avoid an extended trailer-like effects sequence. Twelve to twenty seconds is enough to reveal the device if the entrance, impossible action and comic interruption all read.

### Threat

Withdraw the camera rather than repeatedly tilting it. A 65–85 mm-equivalent compression from behind shoulders makes the far road close in. Foreground heads and a gatepost can obstruct the boys’ view. The important progression is **clear geography → partial obstruction → no easy exit**, not arbitrary shaky movement.

A single axial push can reveal that the boy’s grin has stopped. Save the tightest eye close-up for the first moment he cannot convert fear into speech. Do not overuse tears and open mouths.

### Rupture

Stay within the witness perspective. Use one concrete visual action that changes the scene, followed by reaction and consequence. Do not assemble an ornamental montage of petrol, tyres, matches and wounds. Do not suggest the victim’s guilt or turn the crowd into one picturesque villain. Graphic material, where required for the hard-R master, needs a shot that demonstrates the event and its cost rather than a prolonged visual attraction.

### Aftermath

Open space again, but hold the frame longer. A ball crosses a familiar patch of ground and Zino does not follow. A clean bucket becomes a surface he cannot look into. At the doorway, hold on Mother deciding how to respond instead of cutting immediately to Zino crying.

## Eighteen actual keyframes — three sheets of six

Each sheet must contain six different **9:16 shot compositions**. The total sheet format can be landscape for six arranged as a row or a three-by-two grid, but each internal frame remains vertical. Add panel numbers outside the pictures in the deterministic PDF/layout, not in AI-generated scene art. Character anchors may guide faces; never use one as the rendered substitute for a different event.

### Sheet One — confidence and the night before

1. **K01 / shots 01–02 — Older Zino at well:** Age fourteen, wet hands trembling in foreground, frightened eyes beyond, Mother's partial doorway presence out of focus. Night; no active fire at home. This is aftermath, not a fiery fantasy image. Mother's question can remain off-screen.
2. **K02 / shot 04 — Age-ten slipper goal:** A ground-level foot/ball/slipper composition, road dust and well/compound landmark establishing the world. Feet and ball must have readable trajectories.
3. **K03 / shots 05–06 — Active story triangle:** Ten-year-olds, Zino talking with hands, Gideon counting the score, Luc objecting fairly; no camera bag, adult facial hair or later school clothing.
4. **K04 / shot 07 — Adult Grand:** Imagined adult with canonical navy suit, short curls, close beard, white open shirt, brown shoes; poised on ordinary roofline, gold ball arc possible. He cannot visibly intervene in the real night crowd.
5. **K05 / shots 09–10 — Age-fourteen night-study geography:** Vocational classroom/wiring or building lesson materials, real readers and the school gate beyond. A functional campus, not ruins.
6. **K06 / shots 11–13 — Maths / friendship:** Age-fourteen trio around one exercise book; Luc holds pencil and small Bible, Zino assists, Gideon beside camera bag. One active exchange rather than three cast portraits.

### Sheet Two — attention becomes danger

7. **K07 / shot 14 — Empty corridor wedge:** Mostly architecture and negative space, one distant turn against the movement, discarded humour replaced by attention. No mystery pursuer.
8. **K08 / shot 15 — Three distinct reactions:** Trio turn at study table; Zino first, Gideon weighing, Luc cautious. Age, clothing, desk and pencils match K06.
9. **K09 / shot 17 — Gate geography:** Wide from school side, boys near gate, road crowd farther away, return route legible. Do not jump into the crowd's centre.
10. **K10 / shot 22 — Sleeve grip:** Hands and cloth specific to these characters, one friend held while Zino looks for the other; gatepost anchors spatial continuity.
11. **K11 / shot 21 — Crowd compression:** Foreground shoulders layer across small boys, level horizon, enough background to retain known exit. Anxiety comes from physical obstruction.
12. **K12 / shot 23 — First witness glimpse:** Boys' POV, anonymous adult accused by a crowd, concrete observable threat; no text identifying him as guilty or an explanatory villain pose.

### Sheet Three — witness, choice, home

13. **K13 / shots 25–26 — Rupture:** Actual approved composite event and hard-R scope, camera on boys' sightline; no sensational overhead panorama, hero shot, implied guilt or rescue.
14. **K14 / shots 24/27 — Grand absent:** Older Zino at edge of frame with ordinary roofline/sky occupying the anticipated Grand space; no gold figure. His face fails to make the story happen.
15. **K15 / shots 29–30 — Both friends leave:** Lateral frame, Zino takes Luc and Gideon away toward the previously established gate. Keep both friends visibly accounted for.
16. **K16 / shot 33 — Well return:** Same position and light as K01, now wider to reveal familiar masonry well, mango and quiet homes. Wet trembling hands provide the match.
17. **K17 / shot 34 — Mother asks:** Dignified controlled two-shot, practical teacher/mother wardrobe, English “What happened?” No blame or melodramatic embrace implied by her pose.
18. **K18 / shot 35 — Story fails / truth starts:** Tight older Zino, eye line to Mother; the expression contains effort to speak, not an attractive tear portrait. “Grand…” becomes “There was a man.”

## Acceptance checks for the critic

- Hide captions. Can a viewer still understand football, story invention, leaving home, studying, gathering threat, witness event and return? If not, the pictures are not doing the work.
- Convert the keyframes to silhouettes. Can one still distinguish the boys and the direction of movement? If not, revise posture, blocking and scale.
- Does each insert belong to a previously established person, object or location? If not, it may be a manipulative thriller shortcut.
- Does the actual event pay off the opening within the pilot? A cold open cannot replace a story.
- Are ordinary humour and ability present before harm? The protagonist needs a life rather than a sequence of reactions.
- Does the violence change the boys and their relationship to the world? Graphic detail alone is not mature storytelling.
- Is the final beat an emotionally specific consequence or decision? A title card after a generic gasp is not an earned cliffhanger.

## What this document does not certify

It does not promise awards, production-budget quality or true camera movement from still images. It sets a falsifiable storyboard standard: actual shot-specific frames, legible geography, a purposeful viewpoint, coherent ages, and suspense produced by character and consequence.
