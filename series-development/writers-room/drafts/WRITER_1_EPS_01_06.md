# GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU — Chapter One Shooting Scripts

## Writer 1 Draft: Episodes 1–6

**Format:** Hard-R animated thriller; vertical 9:16; English and Cameroon Pidgin with English subtitles.

**Draft boundary:** Confirmed biographical facts are identified in each episode. Scene order, dialogue, individual landlord visits, and interactions are proposed dramatization unless Zino has supplied them as a memory. Grand-Chukuchuku sequences are always fantasy, not biography.

# EPISODE 01 — “GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU NEVER LOSES”

**Target runtime:** 2:25

**Fact tier:** Confirmed setting and character facts: Zino is ten around 2000, after primary school; he lives in Fiango with his mother; the compound has a well, mango tree, tenants, and a monthly landlord collection for rent and electricity; Zino invents Grand-Chukuchuku stories; he plays football with Luc and Gideon. All action and dialogue below are proposed composite.

**Logline:** A disputed football call lets ten-year-old Zino turn defeat into a Grand-Chukuchuku legend, until the landlord’s visit reveals a problem fantasy cannot solve.

### 0:00–0:16 — COLD OPEN: THE COMPOUND

**VISUAL:** Vertical whip-pan from a bucket rising out of the well to a football skidding across red dirt. The mango tree fills the upper third of frame; tightly grouped houses circle the yard. Doors open. A tenant cooks. Another marks a light-bill figure in a notebook. Zino dribbles through the frame, grinning.

**SOUND:** Ball thuds; metal bucket squeaks; distant radio; children calling in Pidgin.

**ZINO (V.O., proposed):** In Fiango, if you make one mistake, the whole compound gets the replay before sunset.

**ON SCREEN:** FIANGO, KUMBA — AROUND 2000.

### 0:16–0:40 — THE DISPUTED GOAL

**VISUAL:** Gideon guards two stones used as a goal. Luc stands beside the line, half referee, half preacher. Zino winds up. His shot strikes the outside of the stone and bounces away.

**GIDEON (proposed):** Outside! No goal!

**ZINO (proposed):** Your eye don rent room inside darkness.

**LUC (proposed):** Speak truth, Zino. Even football get commandment.

Zino points at the stones, turning a missed shot into a courtroom argument. He sees several younger boys watching.

**ZINO (proposed):** Grand-Chukuchuku score from worse place than this. Goalkeeper beg make he no shoot again.

### 0:40–0:58 — FANTASY: THE IMPOSSIBLE STRIKE

**VISUAL — FANTASY:** The compound stretches into a packed stadium. GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU, a taller idealized version of Zino in exaggerated football boots, leaps from the mango-tree branch. He bends a shot around a goalkeeper, a church roof, and the moon. The ball lands in a net made of gold thread. The crowd explodes.

**SOUND:** Stadium roar cuts abruptly into Gideon shouting.

**GIDEON (O.S., proposed):** Zino! The ball don enter Mama’s washing!

### 0:58–1:20 — THE REAL INTERRUPTION

**VISUAL:** Back in reality, the ball sits in a basin. Zino retrieves it, embarrassed. The landlord enters the compound carrying a small notebook. The energy changes. Doors that were open become careful.

Zino’s mother enters in the background with primary-school exercise books under one arm. She notices the landlord before Zino does.

**LANDLORD (proposed):** Rent and light bill. I go round first.

**SOUND:** The football stops. A pot lid settles. The landlord’s pen clicks.

### 1:20–1:48 — WHAT ZINO SEES

**VISUAL:** Zino watches his mother straighten her clothes and greet the landlord. She does not look afraid, but she folds one hand over the exercise books. Gideon nudges Zino, trying to restore the game.

**GIDEON (proposed, low):** Grand-Chukuchuku fit pay light bill too?

Zino starts a sharp reply, then looks at his mother again.

**ZINO (proposed):** He fit pay for the whole street.

Luc looks at him; he hears the boast differently now.

### 1:48–2:14 — THE FIRST PRIVATE PROMISE

**VISUAL:** Interior, later. The mother marks primary-school books at a table. Zino works beside her, attempting a maths exercise. Grand-Chukuchuku appears in the window reflection, holding a glowing house with every room lit.

**ZINO (proposed):** One day I go build you house. Light no go ever off.

**MOTHER (proposed):** Then start by finishing your own work.

Zino solves the sum. She checks it and nods once. The fantasy house flickers out; the real room remains.

### 2:14–2:25 — HOOK

**VISUAL:** A shadow crosses the doorway: the landlord returns from the other rooms. Zino covers his exercise book with both hands, as if a correct answer can protect the table.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**END CARD:** EPISODE 02 — “RENT AND LIGHT.”

**Episode fact boundary:** The compound, monthly collection, football friendship, mother’s primary-school work, and Zino’s house dream are confirmed. The game, landlord timing, exact maths exercise, and dialogue are proposed composite.

# EPISODE 02 — “RENT AND LIGHT”

**Target runtime:** 2:30

**Fact tier:** Confirmed: the landlord collects monthly rent and electricity money; Zino’s mother is a primary-school teacher; the compound includes multiple households; Zino fears never building his mother a house. Proposed: this single rent day and every spoken line.

**Logline:** On rent day, Zino tries to make his mother’s worry disappear with jokes and schoolwork, discovering that intelligence earns pride but cannot settle an adult bill.

### 0:00–0:20 — THE NOTEBOOK MAKES ITS ROUND

**VISUAL:** Match cut from the landlord’s notebook to close details around the compound: coins counted into a palm; a tenant closing a door; a bare electric bulb flickering; a young student pretending not to hear his name called. The landlord writes, waits, then moves on.

**SOUND:** Pen scratches, low adult voices, electric hum.

**ZINO (V.O., proposed):** In the compound, rent day had one sound: people talking quiet.

### 0:20–0:47 — ZINO TRIES TO FIX THE MOOD

**VISUAL:** Zino returns home holding a football under one arm. His mother continues marking books, her face controlled. He performs a ridiculous Grand-Chukuchuku story with his pencil as a microphone.

**ZINO (proposed):** Grand-Chukuchuku see landlord once. He give the man key to fifty houses and say, “Choose one. Free.”

His mother nearly laughs, but the smile fades when she looks at paperwork outside frame.

**MOTHER (proposed):** Grand-Chukuchuku has too much mouth.

### 0:47–1:15 — HER WORK, HIS WORK

**VISUAL:** She places a primary-school exercise book beside Zino’s own work. Her pupils’ handwriting is younger and less certain. Zino notices that she corrects every page with care even while the landlord waits somewhere outside.

**MOTHER (proposed):** You know this answer?

Zino does. He explains it quickly, with a flourish. She corrects one mistake in his working.

**MOTHER (proposed):** Being quick is not the same as being careful.

**VISUAL — FANTASY FLASH:** Grand-Chukuchuku solves a giant equation by kicking numerals into a goal. A red correction mark cuts through the fantasy.

### 1:15–1:52 — WHAT THE BILL CANNOT SAY

**VISUAL:** From Zino’s point of view, the edge of the landlord’s notebook appears at the doorway. We do not show numbers or claim a specific amount. His mother gets up, tells Zino to continue working, and steps outside.

Zino’s concentration breaks. The words outside remain indistinct. He hears “light,” “month,” and his mother’s controlled “I understand.”

**SOUND:** Pencil stops. A football game resumes faintly beyond the compound.

### 1:52–2:20 — A SMALL, REAL WIN

**VISUAL:** Zino returns to the problem. He slows down. He corrects the line his mother pointed out. When she comes back, he turns the book toward her without a joke.

She reads. This time she smiles fully.

**MOTHER (proposed):** Correct.

Zino absorbs that word as if it is applause in the fantasy stadium. Grand-Chukuchuku appears at the edge of frame, then removes his own crown and places it beside the exercise book.

### 2:20–2:30 — HOOK

**VISUAL:** Zino sees an old photograph or family object placed high in the room. He looks at it, then at his mother. Do not identify it as his father without a confirmed object or family memory.

**ZINO (V.O., proposed):** I did not know every problem had an answer. I only knew I had to become one.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**Episode fact boundary:** This episode may use a photograph or object only after Zino identifies a real family object. The rent-day structure and dialogue are proposed; no bill amount or specific payment result is established.

# EPISODE 03 — “GIDEON’S FIRST CAMERA”

**Target runtime:** 2:35

**Fact tier:** Confirmed: Gideon is business-minded, bought his first camera when he entered secondary school, often paid when out with Zino, and valued Zino’s ease speaking with girls; Gideon was quiet and girls could find him boring. Proposed: timing of the outing, girl characters, dialogue, and camera business.

**Logline:** Gideon’s first camera turns a friendship built on jokes into a small business partnership—until Zino’s habit of speaking for others makes Gideon feel invisible.

### 0:00–0:23 — A TOOL, NOT A TOY

**VISUAL:** Later in the Fiango day-school years. Gideon unwraps a camera from cloth beneath the mango tree. The boys crowd around it. He handles it with more care than the football.

**GIDEON (proposed):** This one no be toy. People go pay for picture.

**ZINO (proposed):** Then take my picture first. My face is investment.

**LUC (proposed):** Pride is free. That one you already get plenty.

**SOUND:** Camera shutter. The image freezes for a beat, then resumes.

### 0:23–0:55 — GIDEON’S PLAN

**VISUAL:** Gideon lists imagined clients with fingers: school events, football teams, couples, celebrations. He frames the mango tree, finding beauty in a place everyone else walks past.

Zino notices Gideon’s focus and changes from clown to partner.

**ZINO (proposed):** You take picture. I bring customers.

Gideon considers this. He knows Zino can start conversations he cannot.

### 0:55–1:27 — THE OUTING

**VISUAL:** Proposed composite. Gideon buys snacks or pays a small outing cost for the group; keep food and location generic until Zino supplies the real memory. Two girls pass. Gideon hesitates. Zino immediately begins speaking for him, making everyone laugh.

**ZINO (proposed):** Gideon say he can take picture that make person look like film star.

**GIDEON (proposed):** I never say that.

**ZINO (proposed):** But you can do it.

The girls laugh at Zino, not Gideon. Gideon’s expression changes almost imperceptibly.

### 1:27–1:58 — THE SHOT HE DOES NOT TAKE

**VISUAL:** Gideon raises the camera to take a portrait. Zino steps into the frame, still performing. Gideon lowers the camera.

**GIDEON (proposed):** You want help me, or you want be me?

The question lands harder than an insult. Luc, who has been watching, closes his Bible.

**SOUND:** The camera strap creaks in Gideon’s hand; background chatter drops away.

### 1:58–2:25 — A NEW RULE BETWEEN FRIENDS

**VISUAL:** Zino hands Gideon the ball, then points toward the girls without moving first.

**ZINO (proposed):** You talk. If your mouth spoil, I go rescue you.

Gideon tries. His first sentence is awkward. The girls wait. Zino holds back instead of rescuing him immediately.

Gideon eventually asks to take their picture. One agrees. The shutter clicks.

### 2:25–2:35 — HOOK

**VISUAL:** Gideon shows Zino the photo. Zino is only a blurred shoulder at the edge; Gideon has centered the subject perfectly.

**GIDEON (proposed):** Next time, stand where I put you.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**Episode fact boundary:** Gideon’s camera, business focus, generosity, quietness around girls, and Zino’s social confidence are confirmed. The portrait opportunity and conflict are proposed dramatization intended to expose the friendship’s real imbalance without assigning false events.

# EPISODE 04 — “LUC’S BIBLE”

**Target runtime:** 2:25

**Fact tier:** Confirmed: Luc is God-fearing, carries a small Gideons Bible, approaches students to preach, values Zino’s directness, humour, maths help, and conversations about girls. Proposed: specific student, maths problem, preaching exchange, and dialogue.

**Logline:** When Luc’s public preaching collides with a maths problem he cannot solve, Zino’s teasing gives way to a friendship in which each boy can name the other’s weakness.

### 0:00–0:20 — THE APPROACH

**VISUAL:** Day school courtyard. Luc carries the small Bible tucked into his hand. He approaches a student sitting alone while other students move toward football and gossip.

**LUC (proposed):** Brother, you get one minute?

The student sees the Bible and tries to leave. Zino intercepts—not to help Luc, but to enjoy the scene.

**ZINO (proposed):** Give him one minute. Luc don prepare sermon like exam.

### 0:20–0:50 — PREACHING AS PERFORMANCE

**VISUAL:** Luc speaks with complete sincerity. Zino acts out the story behind Luc’s back, winning laughter from nearby students. Luc catches him and continues anyway.

**LUC (proposed):** You can laugh. But you still know when your heart no quiet.

For a moment Zino has no answer. Then the bell interrupts.

### 0:50–1:22 — THE MATHS PROBLEM

**VISUAL:** Inside a classroom, Luc stares at a maths problem. Zino finishes his own work early and leans over.

**ZINO (proposed):** You want save everybody, but this number don defeat you?

Luc slides the book across. Zino explains—not with a lecture, but by drawing a football pitch in the margin and turning each step into a pass.

**SOUND:** Pencil scratch; teacher’s shoes approach, then move away.

### 1:22–1:52 — LUC’S RETURN PASS

**VISUAL:** Luc understands and solves the next line alone. He watches Zino return to his own work, looking unconcerned.

**LUC (proposed):** You always talk first because you no want anybody ask how you feel.

Zino looks at him sharply.

**ZINO (proposed):** You preach to calculator now?

**LUC (proposed):** Even calculator get truth.

### 1:52–2:15 — THE JOKE BECOMES A QUESTION

**VISUAL:** Outside, boys debate girls, football, money, and who will be important first. Zino speaks for everyone. Luc watches with warmth and concern.

**LUC (proposed):** One day, your mouth go carry you enter problem.

**ZINO (proposed):** Then you pray me out.

Luc smiles, but does not promise.

### 2:15–2:25 — HOOK

**VISUAL:** A staff member calls a student away from class. The student leaves carrying a bag. Zino watches, suddenly quiet.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**Episode fact boundary:** Luc’s Bible, preaching, faith, and relationship with Zino are confirmed. The student and scene construction are proposed. Do not identify the student as being sent away for fees without Zino confirming this particular memory.

# EPISODE 05 — “SCHOOL FEES”

**Target runtime:** 2:40

**Fact tier:** Confirmed: Zino was sent away from primary school and high school because fees were unpaid; his mother was a primary-school teacher; his paternal uncle helped financially at times. The date, exact school, staff member, words, route home, and resolution of the high-school incident remain unresolved. This entire episode is a proposed composite pending those details.

**Logline:** When unpaid fees make Zino’s place in school conditional, the boy who always has an answer discovers the difference between being brilliant and being allowed to remain.

### 0:00–0:24 — THE ANSWER

**VISUAL:** A day-school classroom. A difficult question hangs on the board. Zino’s hand rises before anyone else’s. Miss Mukete is not used here unless chronology confirms she taught this class; use a neutral teacher silhouette.

**TEACHER (proposed):** Zino.

Zino explains the answer. His confidence is earned. Classmates look to him.

**SOUND:** Chalk snaps once, then continues.

### 0:24–0:55 — THE INTERRUPTION

**VISUAL:** A staff member appears at the door with a paper or list. The teacher’s face changes. Keep the paper unreadable; do not invent amounts.

The teacher asks Zino to step outside. He takes his book as if this is routine.

**ZINO (proposed):** I do something?

No one answers immediately.

### 0:55–1:30 — THE LINE HE CANNOT ARGUE WITH

**VISUAL:** In the corridor, the adult explains that his fees have not been paid. Zino begins with charm, then logic, then anger. Each fails.

**ZINO (proposed):** I write the test. I finish the work. Why I have to go?

**STAFF MEMBER (proposed):** It is not about your work.

That is the cruelest possible answer. Grand-Chukuchuku appears behind Zino in fantasy, ready to tear the paper in half. Zino looks away. The fantasy dissolves.

### 1:30–2:02 — THE WALK HOME

**VISUAL:** Zino walks through Kumba with his bag. The camera stays vertical and close: shoes, dust, a passing football, a bus or vehicle only if confirmed later. He rehearses jokes to tell his mother, then loses them.

**SOUND:** School bell continues after he is gone; Pidgin conversations pass around him without reaching him.

### 2:02–2:29 — THE ADMISSION

**VISUAL:** His mother is marking primary-school work. He stands in the doorway too early in the day. She understands before he speaks.

**MOTHER (proposed):** Why you come home?

Zino begins a Grand-Chukuchuku excuse. It dies in his mouth.

**ZINO (proposed):** They say I no fit stay.

She makes space beside her. Neither person knows, in this draft, how the problem is resolved; do not supply rescue money or dialogue from the uncle.

### 2:29–2:40 — HOOK

**VISUAL:** Zino sees his mother’s primary-school papers, his own schoolbook, and the landlord’s notebook from Episode 1 in a visual rhyme. Three forms of education and money occupy one table.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**Episode fact boundary:** The fee exclusion is confirmed. This sequence is not a transcript of either the primary-school or high-school event. It must be replaced with Zino’s actual age, school, route home, adult words, and resolution before recording voices.

# EPISODE 06 — “MISS MUKETE”

**Target runtime:** 2:35

**Fact tier:** Confirmed: Miss Mukete is Zino’s favorite chemistry teacher; Zino is academically bright; he has a temper and a need to please others. The classroom event, experiment, timeline, dialogue, and any relationship to Elianne are proposed until confirmed.

**Logline:** Miss Mukete recognizes Zino’s chemistry talent but makes him choose between performing for the room and doing the disciplined work that could change his future.

### 0:00–0:24 — A DIFFERENT KIND OF FIRE

**VISUAL:** Day-school chemistry room. Sunlight hits glassware; diagrams of atoms and compounds appear on a board. Zino enters late or theatrically only if chronology permits; in this draft he enters with friends and immediately draws attention.

**SOUND:** Glass clinks; class murmurs; a fan ticks overhead.

**MISS MUKETE (proposed):** Chemistry is not magic. It is attention.

Zino repeats the phrase quietly, amused, then becomes interested despite himself.

### 0:24–0:58 — THE PROBLEM ON THE BOARD

**VISUAL:** Miss Mukete presents an unfinished chemical equation or conceptual question without specifying curriculum content that Zino has not supplied. Students hesitate. Zino has the answer but waits for the room to notice him.

Gideon whispers an incorrect guess. Luc mutters a prayer with comic seriousness. Zino smiles.

**ZINO (proposed):** Chemistry no be prayer. It is balance.

Luc gives him a look: not every truth needs an audience.

### 0:58–1:30 — RECOGNITION WITH A PRICE

**VISUAL:** Miss Mukete calls Zino to the board. He explains the answer well, but adds a flourish that makes classmates laugh. Miss Mukete does not praise the flourish.

**MISS MUKETE (proposed):** You know the answer. Good. Now learn the discipline not to waste it.

The class quiets. Zino feels exposed, then challenged.

**VISUAL — FANTASY:** Grand-Chukuchuku mixes glowing liquids that turn into trophies. Miss Mukete taps the imagined glass; the trophies collapse into ordinary notes and revision pages.

### 1:30–2:02 — THE TEMPER TEST

**VISUAL:** A classmate makes a comment about Zino’s background, fee situation, or intelligence only if Zino confirms a real form of teasing. In this draft, use an unnamed slight without content. Zino rises, ready to fight or humiliate the classmate verbally.

Miss Mukete catches his eye before he acts.

**MISS MUKETE (proposed):** Sit. Finish the work.

He sits, furious. The decision is small but costs him his public image.

### 2:02–2:25 — A STANDARD IS SET

**VISUAL:** After class, Miss Mukete returns Zino’s exercise book with a small correction and one line underlined: not a grade, not invented praise. She asks a question that invites effort rather than rescue.

**MISS MUKETE (proposed):** What do you want this mind to do for you?

Zino watches Gideon and Luc outside, then sees his mother’s house in a brief Grand-Chukuchuku fantasy.

**ZINO (proposed):** More than pass one class.

### 2:25–2:35 — HOOK

**VISUAL:** A notice announces the coming exam period or Professional School without naming a specific date. Students gather around it. Zino folds the corner of his chemistry book carefully.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**Episode fact boundary:** Miss Mukete’s importance and Zino’s academic ability are confirmed. The lesson, correction, classmate slight, and dialogue are proposed. Do not make Miss Mukete a financial rescuer or connect her to a specific result until Zino gives that memory.
