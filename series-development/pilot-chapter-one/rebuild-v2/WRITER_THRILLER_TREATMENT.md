# GRAND-CHUKUCHUKU — Pilot v2 writer treatment
## “THE MAN WHO NEVER RAN”

**Proposed runtime:** 5:00 / 36 shots. Original adult animated thriller. Vertical 9:16.

**Engine:** Zino invents a man who can stop a whole crowd. Four years later, he confronts a real crowd and discovers that his first responsibility is the two friends beside him. Getting them out means abandoning the fantasy of being the man who saves everyone.

**The thriller question:** What will this gifted, justice-minded boy do when the whole street says something terrible is right?

**The episode's irreversible movement:** Storytelling protects Zino's pride at ten. At approximately fourteen, he cannot make the same story protect anyone. He chooses the tangible act of taking his friends home; the choice cannot erase what happened to the accused man or restore his former certainty.

## Three acts

### ACT I — He can talk his way out of anything (0:00–1:15)

Twelve seconds at approximately fourteen: a wet trembling hand cannot grip a pencil. Mother asks “What happened?” No answer. A clear FOUR YEARS EARLIER card takes us to ten-year-old Zino and a scuffed football. A lifted chin, a dare, and a story make Zino someone worth watching. We see Grand in a single impossible feat: a composed navy-suited man makes the roar of a fictional stadium-sized crowd fall silent with one raised palm. This is not supernatural reality: Zino's mouth and the boys' interruption explicitly bracket the fantasy. His sentence is the pilot's promise and warning: **“Grand-Chukuchuku no ever run.”**

A clear FOUR YEARS LATER — EARLIER THAT EVENING card returns to the approximately-fourteen-year-old timeline. Hands that once won a football argument work a maths problem under fluorescent lights. Gideon counts a small camera-job deposit; Luc has brought his small Bible and his unfinished maths. Professional School has tools and construction diagrams, establishing its real daytime function. The normality matters: these are boys living, not symbols waiting for trauma.

### ACT II — The street answers (1:15–3:25)

Zino enjoys an answer, Gideon a business plan, Luc a small willing audience. The friends want different things, and the study night makes room for all three. An accusation interrupts from beyond the classroom. **“Thief!”** The word is first heard without a visible source; no camera supplies confirmation.

They move toward a gathering because curiosity, noise and the flow of other students pull them there. Camera geography remains simple: school gate behind them, crowd in the road ahead, exit at their left. A few steps become a commitment they did not intend. Through adult shoulders the accusation becomes violence. A fallen shoe, a kicking leg and the accused person's open hand provide direct fact-of-harm images without assigning an identity or guilt. Gideon raises his camera instinctively but cannot make himself take a picture. Luc's lips move without sound. Zino's old Grand line starts and fails.

Show the tires, fuel and ignition as brutal material reality; do not give them slow-motion glory, a chanted montage or an explanatory villain. The fire is not the climax of an action ride. The climax is Zino having to move while everyone else remains transfixed.

### ACT III — The first honest act (3:25–5:00)

The imagined Grand position is empty. Zino chooses the exit already established. He grips Gideon's wrist, then takes Luc's shoulder; the three retreat together. They do not abandon a realistic rescue opportunity the script invented solely to condemn them. They are schoolboys escaping a violent crowd they cannot control.

At the same well, water fails to remove the smoke smell. The sound of the football game is now distant and wrong. Mother carries the same workbooks, then sets them aside and sees him properly. The opening trembling hand returns, now understood. She asks what happened. Zino begins the protective lie—“Grand-Chukuchuku...” —and cannot finish it. He has reached a truth he cannot turn into entertainment. He finally says **“There was a man.”** Hold his face; cut to black before he can hide behind another story. The pilot promises the full account and its aftermath without inventing a new threat.

This ending opens the chapter's justice theme without inventing a conspiracy or victim identity. Next episode must pay off his answer and the family's response, then recover ordinary life through the residue of what he saw.
